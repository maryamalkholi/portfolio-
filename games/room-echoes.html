Room Echoes

A ghost haunts a room filled with memories. You can’t see it — you have to listen for what it left behind.

Unity · 6DoF VRExploration / PuzzleDesign & Development

Room Echoes is a narrative-driven VR exploration and puzzle game where players enter a memory-filled room haunted by an invisible ghost. Unable to rely on sight alone, players must use sound, environmental clues, and spatial awareness to locate hidden memory objects and recover three out of five before the ghost makes them disappear. Once enough memories have been reclaimed, the player must confront the ghost directly and use a mystical blade to finally release it.

The Core Design

The central mechanic of Room Echoes is finding something you cannot see.

The ghost is completely invisible, forcing the player to rely on sound, spatial awareness, and environmental clues. Rather than simply searching an area until an enemy appears, the player has to investigate the room and interpret the information the ghost leaves behind.

The ghost itself also challenges the traditional portrayal of ghosts. Instead of being completely intangible and silent, it physically exists within the environment. It can collide with obstacles and produce sounds, allowing the player to gradually determine where it is.

At the same time, the player is racing against it. The ghost can pass through the memory objects and make them disappear, meaning the player must locate and recover enough memories before they are lost.

The Room as a Memory

One of the core ideas behind Room Echoes came from designing my own room.

While arranging it, I started noticing how many of the objects around me represented parts of who I was. Some reflected my interests, some carried memories, and others had sentimental value simply because of what they meant to me. The room started to feel like a physical representation of its owner.

In Room Echoes, the room is more than a level. Its objects are echoes of the person who once inhabited it. The memories attached to those objects are fragments of that person’s identity, while the ghost represents what remains behind. This is where the name Room Echoes comes from: the room contains echoes of a person who is no longer fully there.

The Idea Behind the Game

The concept came together from two experiences I was having at the same time: discovering VR for the first time and designing my own room.

When I first interacted with VR, I was surprised by how heightened my senses felt. Sounds became much more noticeable, the space around me felt tangible, and I became aware of details I would normally ignore in a traditional game. That made me wonder: what if a game made those senses the player’s primary tool?

The idea of an invisible ghost came from that question. Instead of simply seeing the enemy, the player would have to sense its presence.

I also wanted to challenge the way ghosts are normally portrayed in movies. They are often invisible or transparent, able to pass through objects without making much of an impact on the physical world. I wanted to reverse that relationship: the ghost would be invisible, but it would still physically interact with its surroundings and make sounds.

What Makes It Interesting

The core experience is a combination of hide-and-seek and detective work. The ghost is always hiding, but it continuously leaves clues through sound and its interactions with the environment. The player isn’t given its location; they have to investigate, listen, connect clues, and gradually narrow down where it is.

This creates a constant sense of anticipation. Every sound could be a clue, every interaction could reveal the ghost’s location, and the player is always racing against it to recover the memories before they disappear. That made Room Echoes one of the projects I genuinely enjoyed playing myself.
