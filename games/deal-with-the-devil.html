Deal with the Devil

Every deal makes you stronger. Every deal takes something from you.

Unity · C#RoguelikeDesign & Development

Deal with the Devil is a fast-paced isometric hack-and-slash roguelike where the player fights through Hell to reclaim their sold soul. When pushed to the brink of death, the Devil offers powerful abilities in exchange for something fundamental to the player. Each deal provides a powerful advantage while introducing a hidden drawback, forcing the player to constantly weigh power against consequence.

The game’s progression revolves around an ever-growing debt. Every time the player sells more of their soul, the amount they must eventually repay increases, creating a cycle where greed makes the player stronger while simultaneously pushing the goal further away.

The Core Design

The central mechanic of the game is the Devil’s Deal. Instead of simply giving the player upgrades, every deal is designed as a trade. The player receives a powerful ability but gives up something in return.

The goal was to make every deal feel tempting while ensuring that its drawback could meaningfully change how the player approaches the game. This creates situations where the player may desperately want an ability while knowing that accepting it will make another part of the game more difficult.

The result is a progression system built around greed, sacrifice, and escalating debt.

The Idea Behind the Game

The idea for Deal with the Devil began through conversations with my younger sister, who reads a lot of books and often shares their stories with me. One particular story and its ideas about choices and consequences stayed with me and eventually became the starting point for this game.

Later, I participated in the Zanga 2025 competition, whose theme was Give and Take. Although I didn’t ultimately choose this idea for the competition, the theme helped the concept fully take shape. The idea of receiving something valuable while giving something meaningful in return became the foundation of the game’s core mechanic.

Since then, the concept has continued to evolve. Every time I pitched it to someone, their reaction made me want to explore it further. I kept asking myself how far the idea of a deal could be pushed and how the consequences could become part of the actual gameplay rather than simply part of the story.

Deal with the Devil became an opportunity to turn that idea into a complete gameplay system — where the player’s desire for power is also what makes their ultimate goal increasingly difficult to achieve.
