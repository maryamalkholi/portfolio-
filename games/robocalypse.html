Robocalypse

Build your robot from discarded junk. Trust your creation in battle — or lose everything you built.

Unity · C#Robot Building / CombatDesign & Development

Robocalypse is a robot-building combat game where players transform discarded components into custom machines and put their creations to the test in battle. Players gather parts from discarded materials and creatively assemble them into their own robots, giving them control over what they build and how they approach combat.

But every battle comes with a risk. Winning allows the player to take components from their opponent and use them to improve their robot. Losing means risking the components they fought to collect themselves. The game therefore turns the player’s own creation into something worth protecting, making every battle a test of both creativity and confidence.

The Core Design

The core of Robocalypse is built around a simple question: how much do you trust the machine you created?

Players collect discarded components and use them to construct their own robot. Instead of receiving a predetermined character or loadout, the player creates their machine based on the parts they have available and the choices they make.

Those choices then have consequences in combat. Winning gives the player access to new components and allows their creation to grow stronger. Losing puts those components at risk.

This creates a progression system based on creativity, ownership, and risk. The more time and effort a player invests in their robot, the more meaningful the decision becomes to put it into battle.

The Idea Behind the Game

The idea for Robocalypse came from watching Bholi with my younger brother.

What inspired me most was seeing the character use creativity to solve problems and protect the environment. His gadgets were made from discarded and reused objects, turning things that would normally be considered garbage into something useful.

That idea stayed with me. I wanted to explore the same concept through a game where the player isn’t simply handed equipment. Instead, they collect discarded components and use their creativity to turn them into something new — a robot of their own.

From there, I wanted the environmental message to exist within the gameplay itself. Reusing discarded objects isn’t just part of the game’s story; it is the foundation of how the player progresses.

That eventually led to the game’s central risk: if you build something yourself, how much are you willing to risk it? Winning lets you salvage parts from your opponent and continue developing your machine. Losing means potentially losing the components you’ve collected. The environmental theme and the progression system therefore work together: something discarded can become valuable, and something valuable can be lost.
